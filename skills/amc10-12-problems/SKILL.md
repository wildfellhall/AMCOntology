# AMC10/12problems

The output must teach contest problem solving. A correct bank with many records can still fail if its problems merely substitute numbers into the lesson's named formula. Read [patterns.md](references/patterns.md), then [calibration.md](references/calibration.md) when building or revising a lesson or bank.

## Establish the mathematical standard

Read the relevant sections of the supplied textbook, including worked and later practice examples; record chapter and page numbers. Compare actual released middle and later AMC problems across algebra, number theory, geometry, and combinatorics. Record the decisive choices and prerequisite combinations. Source access failures must be explicit; an AoPS index or search snippet is not a substitute for reading a problem. Use official MAA papers or a licensed archive when needed. The supplied textbook also includes AIME and olympiad material: distinguish enrichment from the AMC target.

Keep optional prerequisite fluency available, but identify it honestly. Assign demand from the solution's decisions, not a template's array position, number size, or the number of algebraic lines. A lesson called advanced must actually teach the recognition, justification, and combination of its advanced methods.

## Author the structure before the numbers

For each problem, specify:

- the mathematical target and necessary prerequisites;
- the decisive representation or construction, including why it is not given away by the stem;
- the tempting approach that fails or becomes inefficient;
- a complete exact solution, with all domain and attainability checks;
- an independent check that does not re-evaluate the same answer formula;
- a structural family identifier shared by mere numerical variants.

A synthesis problem must require useful interaction between ideas: for example, transform roots and apply a domain restriction; combine a prime valuation with a residue condition; prove similarity before using circle power; or choose a state representation before solving the recurrence. Naming two topics does not establish synthesis.

Use five distinct answer choices for original AMC-style MCQs. Derive each wrong choice from an identifiable error or plausible incomplete approach. Do not automatically pad choices with answer ± 1, twice the answer, or unrelated formulas. Verify algebraic equivalence as well as textual uniqueness. Avoid wording that makes the correct method conspicuously longer or more qualified than every distractor.

Calculate the result of each stated distractor error. A rationale is incorrect if its proposed mistaken computation produces a different number from the option, even when the correct answer is verified. Keep equivalent formulas and relevant special cases out of competing formula choices; distinct notation alone does not make distinct answers.

## Build lessons and sets with transfer

Use a coherent instructional progression: an entry problem that creates a need for the idea; an explicit derivation with its conditions; a fully explained worked example; a second example that changes representation or combines prerequisites; a completion task with some support removed; and independent transfer. Explain why a first move is productive, not just what calculation follows it. Include a genuine alternative approach or a precise counterexample to a common error where it clarifies the method.

For a 15-item contest lesson, a useful editorial starting point is two fluency checks, four connections, six contest applications, two unfamiliar transfers, and one verified released reference. Adjust for an explicitly labeled prerequisite lesson. Do not present this mix as an empirically validated dosage. Numerical variants can support fluency; they do not fulfill distinct-structure or transfer requirements. Do not reuse a worked-example family as the only evidence of mastery.

Recognition hints point to an observation; setup hints help establish a model. Neither should reveal the entire solution immediately. A solution must identify the key inference, justify the steps, reject invalid candidates, and answer the exact question. A derivation cannot be replaced by a list of formulas.

## Separate the three kinds of practice

**Formula drill:** choose a symbolic relation from a situation, identify its meaning or conditions, or match uses to formulas. It must not quietly become numerical problem solving. Contexts and distractors must be compatible with the selected AMC track. Test the actual route, session mode, question kinds, and displayed heading from every entry point.

**Method selection:** offer plausible competing plans for a substantive problem. State the criterion when more than one plan could work. Explain why each incorrect plan fails for this problem; generic absurdities do not test strategic discrimination.

**Full problems:** require the student to produce the mathematical solution. Formula/method recognition and supported attempts are not independent full-problem mastery evidence.

## Verify and calibrate

Use exact rational/symbolic arithmetic where possible. Independently enumerate finite counting spaces, compare recurrences with direct enumeration, verify geometry by a second coordinate or synthetic derivation, and prove extrema with attainable equality cases. For parametric families, exercise every emitted parameter and boundary. Reject ambiguous conditions, duplicate answers, unattainable optima, invalid triangles, and hidden zero denominators.

Persist the exact answer, solution rationale, demand rationale, prerequisites, family, provenance, and independent verification method. Render every TeX string. A validator that checks counts and parses TeX establishes software integrity, not mathematical rigor. Review samples from every demand band against released benchmarks, and revise the underlying authoring rule when a high-demand label is unjustified.

Maintain stable IDs only when the mathematical item is unchanged. Preserve removed questions separately when needed to load existing sessions and backups. Never reinterpret an old saved answer against a new question with the same ID.

Reordering answer choices changes saved answer indices and therefore requires a new item version. Preserve the structural family when only choices change. Diagnostics must reference the same canonical item used in practice, rather than creating a second ID that can inflate mastery evidence.

Keep attribution explicit: `original`, `adapted` with source and changes, or `released` with verified contest identity and URL. Link to copyrighted released statements unless redistribution permission is available. No publication is authorized by this skill.
